# Test Locations

**Confidence note on coordinates**: these three locations are well-documented in public reporting/literature as water→land-use-change cases. Exact bounding boxes below are my best approximation from public references, not independently surveyed this session — per the "do not hallucinate" constraint, treat coordinates as **candidates to visually confirm** (via the Phase 3 notebook or a quick map look) before using them in any demo, not as ground truth. Confidence is marked per location below.

## 1. Pallikaranai Marsh, Chennai, India

- Approx. bbox: `80.19, 12.90, 80.23, 12.95` (WGS84 lon/lat)
- Story: one of South India's last remaining wetlands, well documented (IIT Madras studies, government reports, multiple news investigations) as having shrunk from roughly 50 km² historically to a small fraction of that due to encroachment, a municipal dumping ground, and IT-corridor construction since the 1990s–2000s.
- Why it's a good test: most of the shrinkage predates Sentinel-2 (2015), so this case tests the **JRC GSW cross-check and "extend further back" fallback**, not just the 2021–2026 Sentinel-2 window — a realistic MVP scenario where the 5-year default window alone shows "no water" for the *entire* range with no visible transition inside it.
- Expected pattern: 2021–2026 Sentinel-2 composites → predominantly built-up/no-water across all years (the loss already happened); JRC GSW historical layer should show substantially larger water occurrence in its 1984–2021 record for the same footprint.
- **Actually tested this session** (full pipeline, see [notebooks/mndwi_validation.ipynb](../notebooks/mndwi_validation.ipynb)): dry-season 2023-11/2024-02 window, clearest scene = 2024-02-04 (5.0% cloud). Result: **38.2% of clear pixels classified as water**, Otsu threshold −0.164. This is plausible for a marsh (partial standing water/wetland vegetation remains alongside encroachment) rather than a clean "all built, zero water" result — consistent with Pallikaranai's known status as a *partially* surviving, still-shrinking wetland, not a fully paved-over site. Good validation that the pipeline produces sane, non-degenerate numbers on a real, messy case.
- **Acceptance run 2026-09-29** (point `12.9385, 80.2246`, 300 m AOI): no water at the point in any readable year 2021–2026 (2024 obscured by cloud), land cover 0% built-up, JRC GSW occurrence 7.8%. The all-dry result **correctly triggered the extend-back fallback**, which probed Landsat 2020/2017/2014 and reported no water at the point there either. Note this is a *point* verdict on a dry part of a marsh that still holds water nearby — AOI-wide Landsat water fraction was 0.36 (2020) and 0.57 (2014), so the marsh itself is clearly wetter historically even though this pixel is not.
- Confidence: HIGH — this is now the pipeline's **extend-back fallback demo**, exercised end to end.
- **MVP app run (2026-09-29)**, point `12.93650, 80.22459` (Nominatim's centroid for "Pallikaranai Marsh"), 2021–2026, dry-season scenes all under 5% cloud: 2021 no water (99%), 2022 water (64%), 2023 no water (67%), 2024 point obscured, 2025 water (96%), 2026 water (80%). JRC GSW occurrence at the point: ~21% (1984–2020). A current building footprint intersects the 60 m check buffer. Reading: a marsh-edge pixel that flips year to year is exactly the seasonal/threshold-noise behaviour [07-limitations-and-risks.md](07-limitations-and-risks.md) #4–5 warns about — the app reports it honestly ("Water detected in 2022, 2025, 2026; no water in 2021, 2023") rather than inventing a disappearance date. For a clean demo narrative, click inside the open-water body, not the centroid.

## 2. Songdo International Business District, Incheon, South Korea

- Approx. bbox: `126.63, 37.37, 126.69, 37.42`
- Story: an entire district built on reclaimed Yellow Sea tidal flat, construction beginning ~2003, widely covered by NASA Earth Observatory and geography/urban-planning literature as a textbook land-reclamation case.
- Why it's a good test: **stress-tests the "no water found in default window → extend further back" requirement explicitly called out in the original spec.** By 2021–2026 (Sentinel-2's window) the area is entirely built — Sentinel-2 alone will show "no water" for every year with no transition visible. Proving the disappearance requires falling back to Landsat (2013+, still mostly post-reclamation) or, more usefully, to JRC GSW's pre-2013 historical water record.
- Expected pattern: Sentinel-2 2021–2026 → no water, buildings present, all years identical (a "flat" timeline — itself a signal the fallback logic should trigger). JRC GSW historical data should show tidal-flat/water presence pre-dating the Sentinel-2 archive.
- **Acceptance run 2026-09-29 (full pipeline, point `37.395, 126.655`, 300 m AOI): the expectation above was wrong, in a useful way.** The timeline is *not* flat — water detected 2021 (confidence 66.7%), then no water 2022 through 2026, with land cover independently calling the point 92.5% built-up by 2023. Summary produced: *"Water body existed through 2021, disappeared by 2022."* So this point caught a real, in-window transition rather than exercising the fallback. Reclamation of the wider district finished long before Sentinel-2, but this particular spot still held water into 2021 — a late-stage basin or canal being filled, not the 2003 landfill. Keep it as the **clean in-window transition demo**; it no longer tests the extend-back path.
- Confidence: HIGH — story widely documented, and the pipeline produced a coherent, independently corroborated transition at this coordinate.

## 3. Manila Bay reclamation area, Pasay, Philippines

- Approx. bbox: `120.96, 14.51, 121.00, 14.55`
- Story: ongoing Manila Bay land reclamation projects (multiple named projects in the Pasay/Parañaque foreshore area), actively covered by Philippine and international environmental journalism since ~2019, converting bay water into reclaimed land for planned commercial/residential development.
- Why it's a good test: **the one location most likely to show a clean transition inside the default 2021–2026 Sentinel-2 window itself** (unlike #1 and #2, which mostly test the fallback path) — a candidate for validating the exact "WATER DETECTED → NO WATER, building appears" year-by-year narrative from the original spec's example output.
- **Actually tested this session** (full pipeline: live STAC search → SAS-sign → windowed pixel read → MNDWI → SCL mask → Otsu) against the bbox above for 2021, 2024, 2025 (2022/2023 had no scene under 30% cloud cover in this AOI): water fraction of clear pixels stayed **~0.55–0.60 in every year tested — no visible transition**. This means the approximate bbox above is centered on open bay water, not tightly on the reclaimed footprint, so it does **not** yet show the expected story. Before using this as a demo case, tighten the bbox to the specific reclaimed-land polygon (visible in any recent satellite view of Pasay's foreshore) rather than the wider bay-mouth area used here.
- **Acceptance run 2026-09-29** (point `14.53, 120.98`, 300 m AOI): water detected in every readable year 2021–2026 (2024 obscured), JRC GSW occurrence 60.8%, land cover 61% built-up in the surrounding area but **not** at the queried point. This **re-confirms the coarseness finding above** — the coordinate sits on open bay water beside the reclamation, so it shows no transition. The summary correctly says so rather than inventing one, and correctly notes the built-up surroundings without claiming the point itself is built.
- Confidence: MEDIUM on the underlying story; the coordinate is **confirmed still too coarse** — tighten onto the reclaimed polygon before using this as a demo. Unresolved, and the one acceptance location that does not yet demonstrate its intended story.
